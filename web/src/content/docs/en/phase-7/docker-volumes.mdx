## The problem

Picture Ana starting PostgreSQL in a <Term id="container">container</Term>, like the nginx from [lesson 1](/en/phase-7/docker-image-vs-container/). She loads the products, creates her account and tries the shop for a week. One day she wants to change the port: she deletes the container with `docker rm -f` and creates it again with the new port.

PostgreSQL starts empty, and the API fails with `relation "products" does not exist`: the table doesn't exist.

It's what you saw with nginx's home page: **what a container writes goes away with it**. A database needs to keep its files somewhere that doesn't depend on the container.

## The analogy

<Analogy>

Think of a furnished rental apartment. When you leave, the owner puts it back the way it was for the next tenant: your things disappear.

So you don't lose your things, you rent a **storage unit** on the side. You move to another apartment and the storage unit is still there, with your key.

There's another way: a door in the apartment that opens onto **a room in your own house**. What you leave there, you see from both sides.

The apartment is the container; the storage unit, a volume; and the door to your house, a bind mount.

<div slot="limits">

- On a Mac or on Windows, a volume lives inside Docker's virtual machine, not among your usual folders.
- Two apartments can share a storage unit. Two PostgreSQL servers writing the same files at the same time ruin the database.
- The storage unit isn't a copy: if you empty it (`docker volume rm`), you lose everything.

</div>

</Analogy>

## How it really works

### Two ways to keep things outside the container

A container writes to its own layer, on top of the image's ([lesson 1](/en/phase-7/docker-image-vs-container/#stopping-starting-and-deleting)). `docker stop` keeps it; `docker rm` deletes it.

For something to survive, Docker mounts a folder from outside at a path in the container. There are two kinds:

| | <Term id="volume">Volume</Term> | <Term id="bind-mount">Bind mount</Term> |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A folder Docker creates and keeps | A folder of yours, whichever you choose |
| How you write it | `-v data:/var/lib/postgresql` | `-v "$PWD":/app` |
| Where it lives | Inside Docker (on a Mac and Windows, in its virtual machine) | On your disk, where it already was |
| What for | Data: a database, uploaded files | Your code while you develop |

Docker tells them apart by what comes before the colon: **a name is a volume; a path**, which starts with `/` or `.`, **is a folder of yours**. After the colon always comes the path inside the container.

### A volume: a folder Docker manages

Volumes have their own family of commands, `docker volume`:

| Command | What it does |
|---|---|
| `docker volume create data` | Creates an empty volume called `data` |
| `docker volume ls` | Lists the volumes |
| `docker volume inspect data` | Shows its details, among them where Docker keeps the files |
| `docker volume rm data` | Deletes it, **with everything in it**. It refuses if any container, even a stopped one, uses it |

You don't need to create it first: if `-v data:/path` names a volume that doesn't exist, `docker run` creates it. And deleting the container doesn't delete the volume: if you don't need it, you delete it yourself.

### A bind mount: a folder from your computer

With `-v "$PWD":/app`, the folder you're in shows up at `/app` inside the container. `$PWD` is the variable with your current folder, the one `pwd` writes; the quotes protect it if its name has spaces.

Nothing gets copied: **both see the same files**. What you save in your editor, the container reads right away, and what the container writes shows up on your disk. If `/app` already had something in the image, the bind mount covers it for as long as it lasts.

That's what it's for: developing in a container with Node 26, with `node --watch` restarting the server every time you save.

**On a Mac, a bind mount is slower:** every read crosses from macOS to Docker's virtual machine. On our Mac, reading the more than 7,000 files in the shop's `node_modules` took 2.1 seconds from a bind mount and 0.1 from a volume. On Linux there's nothing to cross, and neither is there on WSL with the project in your Linux folder (`~`).

### Where PostgreSQL 18 keeps its data

Each <Term id="image">image</Term> decides where its program writes, and the volume has to go right there. In the `postgres:18` image, **the volume goes at `/var/lib/postgresql`**. Inside, PostgreSQL creates its folder with the version number, `18/docker`. The [image's documentation](https://hub.docker.com/_/postgres) says so, and it changed in 18: up to 17 it went at `/var/lib/postgresql/data`, the path you'll see in almost every tutorial.

The image knows that folder is for data: it declares a volume there. If you start PostgreSQL without `-v`, Docker creates an **anonymous volume**, with a name of 64 random letters and numbers, like the ones you may see in your `docker volume ls`. A new container doesn't reuse it: it creates another one, empty. That's what happened to Ana.

The variables `POSTGRES_USER`, `POSTGRES_PASSWORD` and `POSTGRES_DB` **are only used when the volume is empty**: they create the user and its database, which stay saved in it.

### Port 5432 may be taken

Your PostgreSQL from [Phase 5](/en/phase-5/#before-you-start-install-postgresql-18) listens on <Term id="port">port</Term> 5432 of your computer. If you publish the container on the same one, Docker can't use it. You have two ways out:

- **Stop yours:** on a Mac, `brew services stop postgresql@18`; on Ubuntu and WSL, `sudo pg_ctlcluster 18 main stop`. You start it again with `start`. That way you can publish on `127.0.0.1:5432`.
- **<Term id="port-publishing">Publish</Term> on another port:** `-p 127.0.0.1:5433:5432`. Outside, 5433; inside, PostgreSQL stays on its 5432. Both live side by side, and it's what this lesson does.

### Backups with pg_dump

A volume isn't a backup: it's the only place your data is. The backup is made with **`pg_dump`**, the PostgreSQL program that writes a whole database as SQL commands, like the ones in [`shop.sql`](/en/phase-5/#before-you-start-the-shops-data). The image includes it, and you run it inside with `docker exec`.

And copying the volume's folder? **Not with PostgreSQL running.** Its files change while you copy them: you'd take some from before a change and others from after it, a copy that might not start. `pg_dump` reads everything from a single <Term id="transaction">transaction</Term>, a consistent snapshot, while the shop keeps serving. Copying the files is only safe with the container stopped.

The goal: PostgreSQL in a container, with the shop's data, which survives deleting the container; then, your code in a container that sees your changes; and a backup of the database.

<TryIt
  title="3. PostgreSQL 18 with its volume"
  cmd={`docker run -d --name db \\\n  -v data:/var/lib/postgresql \\\n  -e POSTGRES_USER=shop -e POSTGRES_PASSWORD=YOUR-PASSWORD -e POSTGRES_DB=shop \\\n  -p 127.0.0.1:5433:5432 \\\n  postgres:18@sha256:74935e72241653ca55e0414067e6d8763aceb8a810eb51b452253ec3dcfc4336`}

Word by word:

- **`--name db`**: the container's name, the same one [lesson 6](/en/phase-7/docker-networking/) will use. The `\` at the end of a line continues the command on the next one.
- **`-v data:/var/lib/postgresql`**: mounts the `data` volume where PostgreSQL 18 keeps its files.
- **`-e POSTGRES_USER=shop`**, **`POSTGRES_PASSWORD`** and **`POSTGRES_DB=shop`**: the user, its password and its database, which are created this first time.
- **`-p 127.0.0.1:5433:5432`**: the container's 5432, on your computer's 5433 and [only for you](/en/phase-7/docker-networking/#with-no-ip-in-front-the-port-opens-to-your-whole-network).
- **`postgres:18@sha256:…`**: the image pinned by its <Term id="image-digest">digest</Term>, like the `FROM` in [lesson 3](/en/phase-7/dockerfile/#from-the-starting-image-pinned-by-its-fingerprint): the same PostgreSQL 18.6 as ours. [Lesson 8](/en/phase-7/docker-registry/#pinning-by-digest-and-letting-a-robot-update-it) tells you why the course pins images this way.

If you get `address already in use`, port 5433 is taken: see “Common mistakes.”

</TryIt>

### Your code, in a bind mount

Now, the API in a `node:26` container that reads your folder. If you still have the `shop` container from [lesson 4](/en/phase-7/docker-multi-stage-build/), delete it (`docker rm -f shop`): it uses the same port.

<TryIt
  title="9. npm run dev, inside a container"
  cmd={`docker run --rm -it --name shop-dev -v "$PWD":/app -w /app \\\n  -e HOST=0.0.0.0 -p 127.0.0.1:3000:3000 node:26 npm run dev`}
  output={`> shop-api@1.0.0 dev
> node --env-file-if-exists=.env --watch src/server.ts

{"level":30,"time":1791956837427,"pid":26,"hostname":"c67501ea2818","msg":"The shop is listening on http://localhost:3000"}`}

- **`-v "$PWD":/app`**: your project, with your `.env` and your `node_modules`, at `/app`.
- **`-w /app`** (*workdir*): the folder where the command starts, like `WORKDIR`.
- **`-e HOST=0.0.0.0`**: the server listens on every address of the container, as in [lesson 3](/en/phase-7/dockerfile/#env-host0000-listening-on-every-address).
- **`node:26 npm run dev`**: the Node 26 image and the command, which starts `node --watch` ([Phase 3](/en/phase-3/node-http-server/)).

The terminal stays with the server: open another one.

</TryIt>

Open `src/app.ts` in your editor and, in the `/` route, change `"API for Ana and Luis's shop"` to `"Ana and Luis's shop, with a bind mount"`. Save. In the first terminal, `node --watch` clears the screen and restarts:

### And whose node_modules is it?

With the bind mount, the container uses **your** `node_modules`, the one `npm ci` installed on your computer. Almost all the shop's packages are JavaScript, which works on any system: that's why `npm run dev` starts. But the TypeScript 7 compiler is a program compiled for your system, and from a Mac it fails inside:

<TryIt
  title="11. A package compiled for another system"
  cmd={`docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/app -w /app node:26 npm run check`}
  output={`> shop-api@1.0.0 check
> tsc --noEmit

…
Error: Unable to resolve @typescript/typescript-linux-arm64. Either your platform is unsupported, or you are missing the package on disk.
…`}
>

`npm ci` installed the macOS `tsc` on your Mac, and the container is Linux. On a Mac with Intel, the name ends in `linux-x64`. **On Linux and WSL, it works:** your `node_modules` is already Linux's.

</TryIt>

The way out: give the container its own `node_modules`, in a volume mounted on top of that folder. It covers yours only inside the container:

<TryIt
  title="12. A node_modules just for the container"
  cmd={`docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/app -v shop-modules:/app/node_modules -w /app \\\n  node:26 sh -c 'npm ci && npm run check'`}
  output={`added 74 packages, and audited 75 packages in 3s
…
> shop-api@1.0.0 check
> tsc --noEmit`}
>

`tsc` writes nothing: the types are fine. `npm ci` installed for Linux in the `shop-modules` volume, and your `node_modules` stays the same. We've removed npm's warnings.

</TryIt>

### A backup of the database

<TryIt
  title="13. pg_dump, with the shop running"
  cmd={`docker exec db pg_dump -U shop shop > shop-backup.sql\nls -lh shop-backup.sql\ngrep Sticker shop-backup.sql`}
  output={`-rw-r--r--@ 1 ana  staff    15K Oct 14 07:47 shop-backup.sql
5	Sticker	300	100`}
>

`pg_dump` writes to its <Term id="stdout">output</Term>, and your terminal's `>` saves it in your folder ([Phase 1](/en/phase-1/pipes-and-redirection/)). Inside there's SQL with the tables, their data and your sticker. We've changed our user to `ana`. Don't upload it to Git: it has the shop's accounts.

</TryIt>

To restore it into an empty database: `docker exec -i db psql -U shop -d shop < shop-backup.sql`. With `-i`, the container reads the file through its <Term id="stdin">input</Term>.

To finish, clean up. **The `data` volume stays**, with your data: [lesson 6](/en/phase-7/docker-networking/) uses it.

```sh
docker stop db && docker rm db
docker volume rm shop-modules
```

## You have already seen it

- **Your PostgreSQL from Phase 5.** Homebrew keeps its data in `/opt/homebrew/var/postgresql@18`, apart from the program: you update it without losing your data. A volume does the same for a container.
- **Phone apps.** When they update, the whole program is replaced, and your data is still there, kept apart.
- **`npm run dev` from Phase 3.** The same `node --watch`, now inside a container.

## Common mistakes

- **Mounting at `/var/lib/postgresql/data` with PostgreSQL 18.** It's the tutorials' path up to 17. The container stops, and `docker logs` says `Error: in 18+, these Docker images are configured to store database data in a format…` and suggests mounting at `/var/lib/postgresql`.
- **`-v data:…` versus `-v ./data:…`.** With a name, a volume. With `./`, the `data` folder wherever you are: if it doesn't exist, Docker creates it, and PostgreSQL writes to your disk, slower on a Mac.
- **The port is taken.** On Linux, Docker says `failed to bind host port 127.0.0.1:5432/tcp: address already in use`; with Docker Desktop, an error that also ends in `address already in use`. Stop your PostgreSQL or use `127.0.0.1:5433`, and delete the container left in `Created` (`docker ps -a`) before trying again.
- **The container doesn't see your changes.** Docker Desktop and Linux tell the container about every file you save; some alternatives don't: with Colima, start it with `colima start --mount-inotify`. On WSL, the project has to be in your Linux folder (`~`), not in `/mnt/c`.
- **`docker volume rm`, `docker volume prune -a` or `docker compose down -v` without thinking.** They delete the data, with no trash can. Make a backup with `pg_dump` first.

## Summary

- A container's layer goes away with `docker rm`; what must survive goes in a volume (`docker volume create`, `ls`, `inspect`, `rm`).
- `-v name:/path` mounts a volume, for data; `-v "$PWD":/path`, a folder of yours, for your code while you develop.
- PostgreSQL 18 keeps its data at `/var/lib/postgresql`, and a new container on the same volume finds it all.
- If your native PostgreSQL takes 5432, stop it or publish the container on another port, like `127.0.0.1:5433`.
- Backups, with `pg_dump` through `docker exec`: a consistent snapshot, without stopping the database.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="To use pg_dump with docker exec: a consistent snapshot without stopping the shop." correct>

`pg_dump` reads everything inside a transaction. If he wants to copy the files, he has to stop the container first.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="That he’d better save the whole container in an image with docker commit.">

`docker commit` saves the container's layer, not the volumes. PostgreSQL's data wouldn't go into the image.

</QuizOption>

</QuizQuestion>

<QuizQuestion
  question="You develop on your Mac with a bind mount. npm run dev works inside, but npm run check fails with Unable to resolve @typescript/typescript-linux-arm64. Why?"
  review="And whose node_modules is it?"
>

<QuizOption text="The image’s Node is a different one, and TypeScript 7 doesn’t work with it.">

The image comes with Node 26, the course's, and TypeScript 7 works with it. The error says something else: the `tsc` package for Linux is missing.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="A bind mount is read-only, and tsc needs to write files.">

A bind mount can be written to, and `tsc --noEmit` writes nothing. The error says a package is missing.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="node_modules was installed on the Mac, with the tsc for macOS." correct>

The container is Linux and doesn't find its version. A volume at `/app/node_modules` gives it a `node_modules` of its own, installed for Linux.

</QuizOption>

</QuizQuestion>

<QuizQuestion
  question="Your Homebrew PostgreSQL is running and docker run with -p 127.0.0.1:5432:5432 says address already in use. What do you do?"
  review="Port 5432 may be taken"
>

<QuizOption text="Stop the Homebrew one, or publish on 127.0.0.1:5433:5432." correct>

The one that clashes is the outside port, your computer's. Inside, PostgreSQL stays on its container's 5432.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="Change the inside one: -p 127.0.0.1:5432:5433.">

The outside one is still 5432, which is taken. And inside, PostgreSQL listens on 5432, not on 5433.

</QuizOption>

<QuizOption text="Remove 127.0.0.1: so Docker looks for another free address.">

Docker doesn't look for another one: with no address, it publishes on all your computer's addresses and opens the database to your whole network. The port is still 5432.

</QuizOption>
