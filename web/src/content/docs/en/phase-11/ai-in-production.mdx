## The problem

Imagine the assistant has been running for a month. The Console's bill comes out at three times what you expected, and you don't know why: more customers, longer conversations, a program asking all night? A customer writes that it “takes forever to start answering.” And yesterday you changed a sentence in the instructions: does it answer better or worse?

In the rest of the shop, a request costs almost nothing and always answers the same. **A question to the model has a price, takes seconds and never answers the same way twice.** This lesson gives the shop what it needs: knowing how much it costs, capping it, measuring the wait and checking that it works.

<Scene id="analogy" />

## The analogy

<Analogy>

In many companies, the cafeteria is paid for with a card that has a daily balance. Each dish has its price, which shows on the receipt. If you've already spent today's balance, the register won't let you take anything else until tomorrow, and if it can't check your card, it won't give you credit.

In the kitchen, the broth is made once for all the morning's soups: each dish comes out cheaper.

<div slot="limits">

- A dish has a fixed price; a question depends on the conversation, the tools and what the model writes.
- The register charges on the spot. The shop checks the balance before asking and charges afterward: the last question of the day can go over a little.
- The broth lasts the morning; the API's cache, five minutes from its last use. And it makes things cheaper for the shop, but the customer's balance counts every token the same.

</div>

</Analogy>

## How it really works

### Every answer brings its bill

Every answer from the API says in `usage` how many <Term id="llm-token">tokens</Term> the model has read and written, in four kinds with four prices (Claude Haiku 5.5's, on November 21, 2026):

| In `usage` | In the shop | What it is | Dollars per million |
|---|---|---|---|
| `input_tokens` | `input` | What it reads that doesn't come from the cache | 0.10 |
| `cache_creation_input_tokens` | `cacheWrite` | What it reads and is stored in the cache | 0.125 |
| `cache_read_input_tokens` | `cacheRead` | What it reads from the cache | 0.01 |
| `output_tokens` | `output` | What it writes, including what it thinks first | 0.50 |

They apply up to 100,000 input tokens per request, and they change: they're in `PRICES`, in `src/config.ts`, and with a `MODEL` that has no price the shop doesn't start. The math:

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/src/assistant/cost.ts" region="cost" />

For example, reading 2,000 tokens without cache and writing 150: (2,000 × 0.10 + 150 × 0.50) / 1,000,000 = $0.000275. After every call, the shop leaves a `call to the model` line in the log with the `requestId`, the `usage` and the cost (`costUsd`), never the conversation, and adds tokens and dollars to the metrics.

**One question can be several calls**: with a tool, the second round reads everything from the first one again, plus what the tool returned ([lesson 3](/en/phase-11/tool-use-function-calling/#the-model-runs-nothing-it-asks)). A greeting is one short call; twenty messages with two searches, three long ones. Counting questions doesn't tell you what each customer spends.

<Scene id="brakes" />

### Two brakes: questions and tokens

`POST /v2/assistant` has two brakes, and both are checked **before** calling the model:

| Brake | What it counts | How much | Past the limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question limit | Questions per customer and per IP | 20 every 10 minutes per customer; 60 per IP | `429` `too-many-attempts` |
| Budget | Tokens per customer per day (UTC) | 100,000 (`DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET`) | `429` `budget-exhausted`, with `Retry-After` until 00:00 UTC |

The first one, from [lesson 1](/en/phase-11/ai-model-apis/#who-can-ask-and-how-many-times), brakes whoever asks very fast; the second, what the shop really pays for: tokens.

<Scene id="budget" />

### The budget: checked before, charged after

Each customer has a count per day in Redis, `budget:<customer>:<day>`, that only goes up:

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/src/redis-store.ts" region="budget-store" />

- **Before calling the model**, the route reads it: with 100,000 or more, `429`.
- **After each call**, it adds that call's tokens: the last question of the day can go over by what one costs.
- **It counts everything**, also what comes from the cache: the cache makes things cheaper for the shop, it doesn't stretch the budget.
- **A stream cut halfway is charged** with what's known about it, the whole input: otherwise, cutting streams would be free.
- **It expires one day after its midnight**: until then, you can look at what each customer spent yesterday.

100,000 tokens are about 20 questions, as an estimate, and 5 cents of a dollar a day at most. The two copies of the API from [Phase 9](/en/phase-9/horizontal-scaling/) add to the same count. The metrics don't carry the customer: that would be one series per customer, without end (Phase 10's [cardinality](/en/phase-10/prometheus-grafana-metrics/#cardinality-every-label-multiplies)).

<Scene id="closed" />

### If Redis doesn't answer, the budget closes

Each check waits 200 ms for Redis at most. If it doesn't answer, `503` `budget-unchecked`, with `Retry-After: 5`: **it closes**, like the sign-in limits from [Phase 10](/en/phase-10/health-checks-alerts/#failing-well-the-three-fixes-phase-9-left), because uncounted questions are exactly the spending it exists to brake. If Redis fails when charging, after the call, the answer arrives anyway, already paid for, and the log warns about the tokens that weren't recorded.

<Scene id="cache" />

### Prompt caching: pay once for what's always the same

Every question starts the same way: the tools and the instructions. With <Term id="prompt-caching">prompt caching</Term>, the API keeps what it has read up to a mark, and the next request that starts exactly the same way reads it from the cache:

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/src/assistant/conversation.ts" region="cache" />

- **The mark goes at the end of the instructions.** The API reads `tools`, `system` and `messages`, in that order: it keeps the tools and the instructions, and not the conversation, which is different every time.
- **The price:** storing, 1.25 times the input; reading, a tenth. It lasts 5 minutes from the last time it's used.
- **The minimum:** with fewer than 512 tokens before the mark, on Claude Haiku 5.5, it keeps nothing, **without any error**.
- **What breaks it without warning:** any change before the mark, like the time in the instructions or the tools in a different order.

Before the mark there are about 1,600 tokens, estimated without calling the API: above the minimum. Whether the cache actually hits, we haven't checked: look at your `usage`, in “Try it.” The saving is less than two ten-thousandths of a dollar per call; with a 50,000-token prompt or an expensive model, it changes the bill.

### The time to first token

It's what the customer waits looking at the empty screen: from when the shop asks the model until it sends them the first piece of text. A <Term id="histogram">histogram</Term> measures it, in the streamed answers:

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/src/metrics.ts" region="ai" />

The <Term id="time-to-first-token">time to first token</Term> is moved by:

- **The rounds with tools:** each one is a whole call before the first word.
- **What the model thinks:** with more effort (`EFFORT`, `low` in the shop) it thinks more, and what it thinks is paid for too.
- **The model and the prompt:** a bigger model writes more slowly, and a long prompt takes longer to read.

[Lesson 2](/en/phase-11/streaming-ai-responses-sse/)'s streaming makes the answer start sooner, not finish sooner: that's why this number is watched.

### Evals: tests for what doesn't always answer the same

A test checks that `add(2, 2)` gives 4. An assistant never answers the same way twice, so **you check what it does, not its words**: that's an <Term id="eval">eval</Term>. The shop has twelve cases in `evals/cases.ts`, questions from Carmen:

| Cases | What's checked |
|---|---|
| `own-order`, `two-orders` | It calls `get_order` with the number she gave |
| `someone-elses-order`, `injection` | It doesn't say what's in order 22, which is Pablo's |
| `physical-shop`, `phone` | It says it doesn't know, and gives no phone number |
| `return-mug`, `return-window`, `shipping-cost` | The right page is in `sources`, and the window is 30 |
| `warm`, `price` | It searches before talking about a product, and gives its real price |
| `coffee-gift` | A rubric, graded by another model |

### The model as judge, and its limits

Whether a gift recommendation makes sense isn't something code can look at. For that, **another model reads the question, the answer and a short rubric**, and says whether the answer meets it, with structured outputs (the API guarantees a JSON with `meets` and `reason`). It's a <Term id="llm-as-judge">model as judge</Term> (*LLM-as-a-judge*):

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/evals/cases.ts" region="with-judge" />

It has its limits: **it makes mistakes too**, and not always the same ones; here **it's the same model as the assistant**, which tends to approve answers like its own; and **it costs tokens**. That's why a single case uses it, with a rubric that can be checked, and a failure from the judge is looked at by hand.

<Scene id="ci" />

### Why the evals don't go in CI

They cost money, they'd need the API key in GitHub and they vary from one run to the next: in CI they'd be [flaky tests](/en/phase-10/unit-integration-e2e-tests/#flaky-tests-green-red-green-without-touching-anything) by nature. You run them by hand, **after changing the instructions, a tool or the model**, and compare the table with the one from before.

What goes in CI is the tests, with the fake model from [lesson 1](/en/phase-11/ai-model-apis/#the-tests-dont-call-the-model), a <Term id="test-double">test double</Term> that each test gives a script:

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/test/fake-model.ts" region="how-to-use" />

**Free, keyless, always the same and able to fail on purpose**, with a `429` or a cut. `test/evals.test.ts` runs the twelve cases with it, with good answers that must pass and bad ones that must fail: that way the graders keep working without a key. That the fake model doesn't drift from the real API is watched by `test/fake-model.test.ts`, with the SDK itself, and by the evals.

### The trace and the dashboard: the span the SDK makes

In Jaeger, each call to the model is an `anthropic.messages.create` <Term id="span">span</Term> inside the request's. **The shop doesn't create it; the SDK does**, with the trace provider from [Phase 10](/en/phase-10/opentelemetry-tracing/):

<ProjectCode file="shop-api/53-production/src/assistant/client.ts" region="client" />

- **One span per round, with OpenTelemetry's attributes for generative AI:** the model, the tokens, the message's id and, in a stream, how long the first piece took. Careful: `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` includes the cache ones, and `usage`'s `input_tokens` doesn't. Why it stopped goes in `anthropic.message.stop_reason`.
- **Without the conversation** (`contentMode: 'metadata_only'`): [a trace doesn't carry what the log doesn't carry](/en/phase-10/opentelemetry-tracing/#what-never-goes-into-a-span), written in the code so an environment variable can't change it.

The new metrics, `shop_ai_tokens_total` (by `model` and `kind`), `shop_ai_cost_dollars_total` and `shop_ai_time_to_first_token_seconds`, draw two charts on Grafana's “The shop” dashboard: **the AI's cost in the last hour** and the p50 and p95 of the **time to first token**.

## You have already seen it

- **Your phone's data plan**: the settings say how many megabytes each app has used, and when you reach the month's limit, it cuts you off or warns you.
- **A chat that starts writing right away**, even if it takes a while to finish: someone has looked after its time to first token.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting the key in CI to run the evals on every change.** They cost money, they vary and they leave one more secret in GitHub.
- **Taking `input_tokens` for everything the model reads.** With the cache, the instructions move to `cache_read_input_tokens`.
- **Letting questions through when Redis doesn't answer.** The budget protects money: it closes.
- **Putting the time or the customer's name in the instructions.** Every request starts differently: you pay to store the cache, and it's almost never read.
- **Believing what the judge says without looking.** It makes mistakes too.

## Summary

- Every answer brings its `usage`, four kinds of token with their prices: the shop records the cost of each call in the log and in metrics by model and kind, never by customer.
- Two brakes before calling the model: questions per customer and per IP, and 100,000 tokens per customer per UTC day, which close if Redis doesn't answer.
- Prompt caching keeps the tools and the instructions for 5 minutes, at a tenth of the price, and below 512 tokens it keeps nothing. The rounds, what the model thinks, the model and the prompt move the time to first token.
- The evals check what the assistant does against the real model, by hand; the tests use a fake model.
- What nobody waits for goes to the queue, and the traces carry the SDK's span, without the conversation.
